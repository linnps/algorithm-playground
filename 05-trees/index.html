Planned interactive demo

Type or paste a sequence of integers. They're inserted in order into three trees — a plain BST, an AVL tree, and a Red-Black tree — rendered side-by-side. The plain BST tilts into a chain; the AVL and RB trees stay balanced via rotations.

What it teaches

  • Why a plain BST is fragile — sorted-or-near-sorted insertions degrade it to a linked list.
  • The cost of balance: AVL rebalances aggressively (more rotations, tighter height); RB allows looser balance (fewer rotations, slightly higher height).
  • Visual proof of why std::map, java.util.TreeMap, etc. all use Red-Black trees rather than AVL — the rebalance cost is what dominates real workloads.
